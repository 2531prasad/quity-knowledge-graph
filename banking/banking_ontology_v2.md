### 1.2 Balance-Sheet Physics, Bottlenecks & Cycle Asymmetries

#### 1. Deposit Mobilization & LDR (Loan-to-Deposit Ratio) Bottlenecks
* **System CASA Depletion:** Between FY22 and late-2026, Indian banking CASA ratios experienced structural compression from historical highs of `~44–46%` down to `~38–40%`. Retail depositors systematically shifted surplus liquidity from low-yielding savings accounts (`2.75%–3.50%`) into high-rate special term deposits (`7.10%–7.60%`).
* **LDR Ceilings & Regulatory Guardrails:** Following mega-mergers and aggressive retail lending runs, system LDR reached `~78–80%`, with several private institutions touching `85%–90%+` (and HDFC Bank peaking `>105%` immediately post-merger). The RBI mandated banks to re-align credit growth with deposit accretion, forcing lenders with high LDRs to decelerate loan disbursements to `10%–12%` YoY and engage in aggressive retail branch mobilization.
* **Term Deposit Repricing Lag:** Even after RBI policy rate adjustments, bank Cost of Funds (CoF) exhibits a sticky `2 to 3 quarter repricing tail` as fixed-term deposits originated at lower historical rates gradually mature and roll over into higher peak-rate deposit brackets.

#### 2. Asset-Liability Repricing Asymmetries (EBLR vs. Fixed Yield Mechanics)
* **EBLR Floating Book (Instant Repricing):** External Benchmark Linked Lending Rate (EBLR) loans (accounting for `~55%–60%` of private bank advances, heavily concentrated in home loans and floating MSME facilities) reprice automatically within 30 to 90 days of an RBI Repo Rate move. In rate-hike cycles, asset yields jump immediately while deposit costs lag, driving transient margin expansion. In rate-cut cycles, yields drop immediately while deposit costs stay elevated, inducing severe NIM compression.
* **Fixed-Rate Books (Sticky Yield / Counter-Cyclical Margins):** Lenders focused on Vehicle Finance (Used/New CVs, Cars, Tractors), Microfinance (JLG), Gold Loans, Consumer Durables, and Fixed MSME maintain contractually fixed asset yields (`11%–24%`). When wholesale funding costs or deposit rates surge, their NIMs compress heavily. Conversely, when system rates plateau or decline, their cost of funds drops rapidly while asset yields remain locked, triggering powerful multi-quarter RoA expansions.

#### 3. Regulatory & Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Friction
* **Universal Commercial Banks vs. SFB Mandates:** Universal Scheduled Commercial Banks are mandated to deliver `40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC)` to priority sectors (18% Agriculture, 7.5% Micro-enterprises, 10% Weaker sections). Small Finance Banks (SFBs) operate under an onerous `75% ANBC PSL mandate`, with a statutory requirement that at least `50% of the loan book must consist of advances <= ₹25 Lakhs`.
* **RBI Unsecured & Microfinance Guardrails:** In late-2026, the RBI firmly reinforced its macroprudential guardrails:
  1. Refused industry appeals to dilute the **50% Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR) cap** on microfinance households (annual income up to ₹3 Lakh), citing high consumption expenditure and multi-lender over-indebtedness risks.
  2. Sustained heightened risk weights on unsecured consumer credit and credit cards (+25% RWA penalty), moderating growth in personal loans.
  3. Enforced tightened Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) norms regarding run-off rates on internet- and mobile-enabled retail deposits.

#### 4. Operating Leverage vs. Credit Cost Trap
* **The High-Yield Unsecured Illusion:** Microfinance NBFCs and MFI-dominated SFBs report nominal yields of `20%–25%` and optical NIMs of `8%–11%`. However, their business model requires dense field-officer branch networks (Cost-to-Income `55%–65%`) and faces extreme tail-risk credit cost spikes (`4%–7%` credit costs during demonetization, Covid-19, and regional farm/climate distress), destroying net RoA.
* **The Low-Yield Secured Compounding Engine:** Prime mortgage and secured MSME lenders operate at optical yields of `9%–12%` and NIMs of `3.2%–4.2%`. However, backed by immovable collateral (LTV `<50%–60%`), digital automated underwriting, and sticky corporate salary CASA, their operating cost stays low (Cost-to-Income `<40%`) and credit costs remain negligible (`<0.30%–0.60%`), enabling uninterrupted `1.8%–2.3% RoA` and `16%–20% RoE` compounding across decades.

### 1.3 Late-2026 Live Sector Brief (November 2026 Macro Regime & Catalysts)

* **Monetary Policy & Inflation Regime:**
  * **Current RBI Repo Rate:** `5.50%` (following the 25 bps hike delivered at the October 2026 MPC meeting, establishing a stance of "calibrated tightening").
  * **Inflation Breach:** Retail inflation (CPI) surged to `6.09%` in October 2026, breaching the RBI's 6.0% upper tolerance band driven by vegetable price shocks and sticky food/dairy components. This has placed a potential follow-on `25 bps hike to 5.75%` on the active radar for the December 2026 / February 2027 MPC reviews.
  * **Liquidity & Credit-Deposit Spread:** Durable liquidity in the banking system remains calibrated. System credit growth is running at `~13.5%–14.0% YoY`, outpacing deposit growth (`~11.0%–11.5% YoY`), sustaining elevated competition for retail term deposits.
* **MFI Stress Cycle & Over-Indebtedness Seasoning:**
  * Microfinance portfolios across SFBs and NBFC-MFIs experienced elevated early-bucket delinquencies (30+ DPD) through 2026, driven by borrower over-leveraging across multiple lenders.
  * The RBI's November 2026 reaffirmation of the 50% FOIR ceiling forced lenders to tighten underwriting filter rates, sharply curtailing disbursements to borrowers with `>3 active MFI relationships` and driving credit costs upwards to `2.5%–4.0%` for un-diversified players.
* **The Gold Loan Structural Boom (+83% YoY Surge):**
  * Regulatory harmonization effective April 1, 2026 established uniform guidelines across commercial banks and NBFCs for gold lending, enforcing disciplined appraisal and repayment tracking.
  * Supported by an unprecedented multi-year gold price bull run (+30%+ YoY), the system gold loan book surged past `₹5.5 Lakh Crore`. Lenders enjoy significant collateral LTV headroom (`<60% effective LTV`), near-zero Loss Given Default (LGD), and rapid portfolio velocity.
* **Major Corporate Actions & Institutional Transformations:**
  * **AU Small Finance Bank:** Completed operational merger with Fincare SFB, creating a pan-India asset footprint across 21 states; submitted formal application to RBI for **Universal Banking License** under the voluntary transition guidelines.
  * **HDFC Bank:** Successfully reduced post-merger LDR from `>105%` toward `98%–99%` via calibrated credit growth (`~11%`) and retail deposit drives; finalized draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) for the mega-listing of **HDB Financial Services** in compliance with RBI Upper-Layer NBFC listing mandates.
  * **Kotak Mahindra Bank:** Completed third-party IT infrastructure audits and submitted comprehensive remediation reports to RBI following the April 2024 restrictions on digital onboarding and fresh credit card issuance; navigating new leadership under MD & CEO Ashok Vaswani.
  * **IDFC First Bank:** Completed legal and structural reverse merger of IDFC Limited into the bank; finalized redemption of high-cost legacy infrastructure bonds, capturing `~300 bps` funding cost arbitrage into core operating profit.
  * **Bank of Baroda & PSBs:** Resolved supervisory restrictions on the 'bob World' mobile application with full onboarding restoration; sustained `1.1%+ RoA` and `16%–18% RoE` driven by a clean corporate credit cycle and negligible wholesale slippages.
  * **Bajaj Finance & NBFCs:** Successfully listed subsidiary **Bajaj Housing Finance Limited (BHFL)** at a premium valuation; expanded cross-sell franchise across `88+ million` customer base while proactively pruning uncollateralized personal digital loans.
