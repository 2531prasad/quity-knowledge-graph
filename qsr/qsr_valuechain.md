### 1. QSR 101, Store Economics, Payback Dynamics & Sector Structural Shifts (Pages 1–22)

#### Value Chain Stages
$$	ext{Raw Agri/Dairy Inputs} \longrightarrow 	ext{Central Commissary / Cold Chain} \longrightarrow 	ext{Front-End Retail Store} \longrightarrow 	ext{Omni-Channel Customer}$$
* **Stage 1: Upstream Farm/Processor:** Poultry integrators (broiler chicken), dairy cooperatives (milk, mozzarella, butter), bakery flour millers (buns, pizza dough), processed vegetable suppliers (potatoes, onions, lettuce), sauces & seasonings, edible frying oils.
* **Stage 2: Midstream Commissary / Logistics:** Central cold storage, flash-freezing facilities, quality inspection labs, temperature-controlled reefer fleet.
* **Stage 3: Downstream Store Operations:** Front-of-house service counter, POS terminals, commercial kitchens (fryers, conveyor ovens, espresso machines), dine-in seating, drive-thru lanes.
* **Stage 4: Last-Mile Delivery / Aggregation:** Proprietary delivery fleet (Domino's 30-min guarantee) vs 3PL FoodTech aggregators (Zomato, Swiggy) charging 18%–20% commission on delivery orders.

#### The Fundamental Restaurant Cost Structure (Pages 6–7)
1. **Setting-Up Capex:** First month advance rent / security deposits, interior fit-outs & civil finishes, commercial kitchen equipment, furniture & fixtures, POS billing & IT hardware.
2. **Operating Cost Stack:** Food inventory / COGS (25%–35%), Store staff salaries & uniforms (10%–16%), Royalty fees paid to global brand parent (3%–6.3%), Store rental / lease occupancy (9%–15%), Utilities & electricity (4%–7%), Local marketing & advertising (3%–6%), R&M, licensing, laundry & cleaning.

#### The 5 Pillars of the SOIC Mental Model for QSR Success (Page 22)
1. **Successful Unit Economics:** Store payback must be $\le 3$ years with healthy store-level EBITDA ($>15\%$).
2. **Zero Concept Risk:** Proven cuisine category (Pizza, Burger, Fried Chicken) with daily/weekly consumption habits rather than fad cuisines.
3. **High Replicability ("Cookie-Cutter Model"):** Ability to deploy standard kitchen setups across Tier 1, 2, and 3 cities with uniform quality without relying on skilled head chefs.
4. **Increasing Innovation / Average Daily Sales (ADS):** Expanding dayparts (Breakfast, Lunch, Evening snacks, Late night) and high-margin product extensions (Café, Desserts, Combos).
5. **Omni-Channel Execution:** Unified balance across Dine-In, Takeaway, Drive-Thru, and Digital Delivery.

### 2. Westlife Development / McDonald's West & South (Pages 23–33)

#### Value Chain Stages
$$	ext{Contract Farmers / Processors} \longrightarrow 	ext{Cold Chain (Radhakrishna Foodland)} \longrightarrow 	ext{McDonald's Restaurants} \longrightarrow 	ext{Omni-Channel Customer}$$
* **Upstream Supply Chain:** Localized sourcing developed over 25+ years (McCain India for French fries/potato patties, Vista Processed Foods / OSI Group for chicken patties, Schreiber Dynamix for cheese slices, Mrs. Bector's Cremica for buns and liquid condiments).
* **Midstream Operations:** Dedicated multi-temperature distribution centers and refrigerated fleet ensuring 100% cold-chain integrity.
* **Downstream Restaurant Formats:** Flagship high-street drive-thrus, mall food court counters, high-density compact metro stores, integrated McCafe kiosks.

#### The McCafe Economic Arbitrage (Pages 29–30)
* **High Gross Margin Profile:** Coffee and hot beverages generate **75%–80% Gross Margins**, significantly above burgers (58%–65%).
* **Incremental Capex Efficiency:** Adding a McCafe kiosk inside an existing McDonald's requires only **₹20 to ₹30 Lakhs** of incremental capex.
* **Payback:** McCafe kiosk payback is achieved in **less than 2 years**.
* **Network Penetration:** Grown from 75 McCafes in FY16 to **273 McCafes by 9MFY23** (~80% of all Westlife restaurants have McCafe).
* **Price Value Benchmark:**
  * **Cappuccino:** McCafe ₹170 vs Cafe Coffee Day (CCD) ₹190 vs Starbucks ₹265.
  * **Latte:** McCafe ₹170 vs CCD ₹199 vs Starbucks ₹265.
  * **Mocha:** McCafe ₹201 vs CCD ₹209 vs Starbucks ₹305.

#### Amit Jatia's Commentary on Burger vs Beverage Economics (Page 33)
> *"If you compare to a coffee QSR or a beverage QSR like Jamba Juice... the gross margin for those people is definitely going to be better (75%–80%), but their rent cost as a percentage of sale will be much higher because their average volumes are lower. Versus burger brands where overall investment is higher to start with and crew is higher, but our average volumes are very high and that gives us the benefit of operating leverage. In India, it is about growing the frequency of eating out... we think in the last two years we have made substantial progress to get to 62%–63% gross margin."*

* **Store Footprint:** 261 stores in Q1FY18 $\longrightarrow$ 304 in Q1FY20 $\longrightarrow$ 311 in Q1FY21 $\longrightarrow$ 331 in Q1FY23.

  * Target **450 to 500 restaurants** across 40+ cities.

  * Top-line sales growth of **2.5x to 3x** with Mid-to-High single-digit SSSG.
  * Expand EBITDA margin to **low-to-mid teens**.

* **Omni-Channel Execution:** Digital-led sales grew **+55%**; cumulative app downloads crossed **21 Million+** with **+22% YoY** growth in Monthly Active Users (MAU).

### 4. Varun Beverages / PepsiCo Bottling & Beverage Proxy (Pages 46–66)

#### Value Chain Stages
$$	ext{PET Resin / Sugar / Concentrate} \longrightarrow 	ext{Automated Bottling / In-House Preforms} \longrightarrow 	ext{Depots / Primary Distributors} \longrightarrow 	ext{8.4L Visi-Coolers / General Trade (89%)}$$
* **Upstream Inputs:** Beverage concentrates (exclusively sourced from PepsiCo), liquid sugar / invert syrup, purified treated water, PET resin chips for bottle blow-moulding, aluminum cans, crowns/closures, corrugated packaging.
* **Midstream Bottling Integration:** Highly automated bottling lines producing Carbonated Soft Drinks (CSD), Non-Carbonated Juices (Tropicana), Bottled Water (Aquafina), Sports Drinks (Gatorade), and Dairy/Energy drinks (Sting).
* **Downstream Cold-Chain Infrastructure:** 8.40 Lakh Visi-Coolers deployed at retail counters, 2,500+ delivery trucks, 2,000+ primary distributors, 100+ strategically located distribution depots.

#### Why Brand Principals Exit Bottling: Kannan Sitaram (Ex-COO Dabur) (Page 50)
> *"A beverage business has two sides: managing the brand and bottling operations. Bottling operations are always capital intensive and it makes sense to hive it off to partners who have lower return expectations. The brand is the high-value part of the business, which they keep with them. In the early days, Pepsi and Coke set up their own bottling plants because local partners lacked the financial might. Now that franchise partners have scale, both companies see merit in exiting bottling to reinvest in brand equity."*

#### The Retail Real Estate Moat: 89% General Trade (Page 51)
* **General Trade (Kiranas):** Generates **89%** of total Indian beverage volumes.
* **Modern Trade:** 10%.
* **E-Commerce:** 1%.
* **Strategic Moat:** In beverages, product availability and cold placement (Visi-cooler real estate inside mom-and-pop stores) determine market share over media advertising alone.

### 1. Jubilant FoodWorks / Domino's Delivery Moat & Scale Economics (Pages 67–75)

#### Value Chain Stages
$$	ext{Contract Dairies / Flour Mills} \longrightarrow 	ext{Central Commissaries (Dough Balls)} \longrightarrow 	ext{Compact Stores (<1,000 sq ft)} \longrightarrow 	ext{Proprietary 30-Min Delivery}$$
* **Upstream Sourcing:** Proprietary cheese formulations (long-term contracts with dairy processors), specialized high-protein wheat flour, canned tomato paste, processed meats.
* **Midstream Commissary Network:** Centralized commissaries manufacture fresh dough balls daily under strict temperature control; delivered to stores via captive refrigerated fleet. Eliminates skilled dough preparation at store level.
* **Downstream Front-End Stores:** Compact, high-throughput delivery-centric stores (average size compressed from 1,200–1,400 sq. ft. down to 800–1,000 sq. ft.).
* **Proprietary Delivery Moat:** In-house delivery riders on electric two-wheelers execute the famous 30-minute delivery guarantee; avoids 18%–20% aggregator commission fees on direct app orders.

#### Global Pizza Market Context & Delivery Dominance (Pages 70–72)
* **Global Pizza Industry:** **$120 Billion** total category; Global QSR Pizza accounts for **$81 Billion** (US QSR Pizza $40B, International QSR Pizza $41B).
* **US Market Saliency:** In the US, the top 4 chains control 52% market share. Domino's holds **22% total US QSR Pizza market share**, **36% US Pizza Delivery market share**, and **48% US Pizza Carryout market share**.
* **Long-Term Same Store Sales Growth (Page 71):**
  * US SSSG: 2011–2021 Average: **+7.0%**.

### 3. Sapphire Foods / Regional KFC/Pizza Hut Arbitrage & Sri Lanka Operations (Pages 93–107)

#### Brand Selection Philosophy (Page 93)
Sapphire's investment mandate criteria:
1. "Center of plate" daily/weekly consumption (meal replacement vs impulse snacking).
2. Difficult to replicate at home (pressure-fried chicken, authentic Italian pizza).
3. Strong brand provenance and global heritage.
4. "Chef-less" operations driven entirely by Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
5. Value-for-money consumer pricing with omni-channel delivery suitability.

#### The Concept Risk Trap: Dunkin' Donuts Case Study (Pages 94–96)
* Brands are often consumer fads; unproven international formats incur extreme capital destruction in India.
* **Jubilant's Dunkin' Donuts Downsizing (Page 95):** JFL was forced to **shut down more than 50% of its Dunkin' Donuts stores** across India after years of continuous losses, converting remaining outlets into low-capex kiosks.
* **Current Concept Experiments:** Devyani testing South Indian QSR format *Vaango*; Jubilant experimenting with Chinese (*Hong's Kitchen*), Biryani (*Ekdum!*), and Cajun fried chicken (*Popeyes*).

#### Sapphire vs Devyani Margin Head-to-Head (Pages 97–98)
*(Source: I-Sec Research)*
* **Gross Margin Parity:**
  * KFC: Devyani 68.5% vs Sapphire 69.2% (9MFY22).
  * Pizza Hut: Devyani 75.8% vs Sapphire 75.6% (9MFY22).
* **EBITDA Margin Gap:** Sapphire's store EBITDA margins historically trailed Devyani by 140–280 bps due to higher metro real estate rental burdens in Mumbai and Delhi, and higher corporate overheads.

#### The Pizza Hut Store Downsizing Miracle (Pages 99, 102)
Sapphire aggressively engineered a turnaround of its Pizza Hut franchise by shrinking store footprints:
* **Footprint Compression:** Average restaurant size reduced by **45%** (from **2,736 sq. ft. down to 1,500 sq. ft. and 1,200 sq. ft.** compact delivery-first formats).
* **ADS & Margin Expansion:**
  * ADS expanded from ₹58,000 (FY20) to **₹62,000 (6MFY23)**.
  * Restaurant EBITDA Margin skyrocketed from **7.2% (FY20) to 14.9% (6MFY23)** (+770 bps margin expansion!).
  * Store count expanded from 174 (FY20) to 249 (H1FY23).
* **Payback:** Slashed payback periods from 6 years down to **~3 years**.

#### Key Risks Specific to Sapphire Foods (Pages 105–107)
1. **Inferior Geographic Mix:** Higher concentration in high-vegetarian states (Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana) leads to severe seasonal demand swings during Shravan and Navratri (Page 106 concall with Vijay Jain).
2. **Private Equity Exit Overhang:** Founded by Samara Capital, Goldman Sachs, and CX Partners; periodic block sales by PE sponsors depress secondary market sentiment.
3. **Sri Lanka Macroeconomic Crisis:** Operates 100+ Pizza Hut and KFC outlets in Sri Lanka; hit hard by 80%+ currency devaluation, soaring local food inflation, and fuel shortages.

#### The Operating Deleverage Threat: Speciality Restaurants Cautionary Tale (Pages 113–115)
* Speciality Restaurants (Mainland China, Oh! Calcutta, Sigree) expanded aggressively between FY12 and FY15.
* When store footfalls dropped slightly, high fixed occupancy and kitchen staff costs triggered catastrophic operating deleverage:
  * Sales grew from ₹140 Cr (FY10) to ₹340 Cr (FY19), but **PAT collapsed from +₹20 Cr in FY12 to chronic net losses (-₹10 Cr to -₹30 Cr annually) from FY16 to FY20**.
  * Stock price collapsed by **>85%**.
* **BBQ Nation's Store Maturation Drag (Pages 116, 118 concall with Rahul Agrawal):**
  * New stores take 24–36 months to mature: Year 1 store margin is only **7%–8% (0% in first 6 months)**; Year 2 reaches **~15%**; Year 3 achieves mature **21%**.
  * With 25% of the portfolio under 1 year old, aggressive expansion into Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities creates a major near-term drag on consolidated return ratios.

### 5. Restaurant Brands Asia / Burger King Barbell Strategy & Indonesia Integration (Pages 120–131)

#### Value Chain Stages
$$	ext{Vegetables / Poultry / Buns} \longrightarrow 	ext{Central Third-Party Cold Chain} \longrightarrow 	ext{Burger King Stores} \longrightarrow 	ext{In-Store BK Cafe / Aggregator Delivery}$$
* **Barbell Product Architecture (Page 120):**
  * **Bottom Anchor (Entry Value):** "Stunner Menu" at ₹50 (veg) and ₹70 (non-veg) drives aggressive young footfalls and trials.
  * **Top Anchor (Premiumization):** Whoppers, Gourmet Kings, and King Fries priced at ₹150–₹250+ capture margin expansion.
* **BK Cafe Kiosk Economics (Page 124):**
  * Scaled from 0 to **180 BK Cafes by September 2022**; integrated into all newly opened restaurants.
  * Delivers an immediate **+7% uplift in Average Daily Sales (ADS)** by unlocking the breakfast and evening coffee dayparts.

#### Contractual Store Expansion & Royalty Structure (Pages 121, 122, 125)
* **Master Franchise Mandate:** Legally committed to scale the Indian store network from 390 stores (FY23) to **700 stores by FY26**.
* **High Royalty Escalation Burden (Page 122):**
  * Domino's pays 3.0%–4.0% royalty.
  * McDonald's pays 4.0%–5.0% royalty.
  * KFC / Pizza Hut pays 6.0%–6.3% royalty.
  * **Burger King pays 5.0% currently, stepping up contractually to 6.3% and escalating up to 8.0% till 2039!** This represents the heaviest royalty drag in the listed Indian QSR space.

#### Key Strategic Risks in Burger King / RBA (Page 131)
